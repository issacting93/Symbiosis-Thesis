# Cognitive & Linguistic Effects

How AI interaction changes how people think, speak, and reason — including language convergence, cognitive offloading, and deskilling. Central to **RQ1** (linguistic metaphors) and supports **RQ2/RQ3**.

---

## Core Papers

- [[Sociocultural Theory — Vygotsky 1934]] — ZPD, scaffolding, cultural tools; the root question of whether AI builds or replaces cognitive capacity
- [[Stance Alignment Affiliation — Stivers 2008]] — nodding as nonverbal affiliation token; stance in conversation
- [[Linguistic Convergence Humans LLMs — Blevins 2026]] — accommodation goes both ways; humans adapt to LLM style
- [[Bidirectional Linguistic Accommodation — Chen 2026]] — progressive interpersonal convergence in human-AI conversation
- [[GenAI Critical Thinking — Lee 2025]] — GenAI reduces cognitive effort and critical thinking in knowledge workers
- [[AI Deskilling — Ferdman 2026]] — AI deskilling as structural problem, not individual failure
- [[Moral Deskilling Upskilling — Vallor 2015]] — technology both erodes and cultivates moral capacities
- Cognitive Overload — Kirsh *(no paper note yet)* — theoretical framework for cognitive overload
- Generative Behaviors Cognitive Models — Fan *(no paper note yet)* — generative behaviors as targets for AI cognitive models

## Key Questions for RQ1
- Do users adopt different vocabulary/metaphors depending on embodiment form?
- Does nonverbal interaction (gesture, expression) change how users conceptualize AI agency?
- Do linguistic patterns reflect embodiment-specific mental models?
