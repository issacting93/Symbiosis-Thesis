# Social Response & Anthropomorphism

How humans project social and emotional attributes onto AI and robots — and how design choices (naming, form, framing) amplify or reduce these responses. Directly relevant to **RQ1** (mental models, linguistic metaphors).

---

## Core Papers

- [[Computers as Social Actors — Nass 1994]] — CASA: humans treat computers as social agents regardless of awareness
- [[Machines and Mindlessness — Nass 2000]] — social responses are automatic, not reasoned
- [[Media Equation — Reeves Nass 1996]] — foundational empirical statement of CASA; social rules applied to all media
- [[Computer Power and Human Reason — Weizenbaum 1976]] — ELIZA effect; emotional attachment from minimal pattern-matching
- [[Who's Johnny — Darling 2015]] — naming and narrative framing shift users' moral consideration of robots
- [[Empathic Concern HRI — Darling 2015]] — backstory/narrative increases empathy toward robots
- [[Future You Digital Twins — Albrecht 2025]] — avatar as future self; identity projection onto virtual AI
- [[Bonding with Couchsurfing Robot — Mollen 2023]] — cohabitation increases bonding; familiarity effect
- [[Companion-Assistant Dynamics — Manoli 2025]] — "better than human" framing; anthropomorphic idealization

## Linguistic Metaphors (RQ1)
Users construct metaphors to describe AI agency. Embodiment form shapes metaphor choice:
- Virtual agents → "helper," "assistant," "advisor"
- Humanoid robots → "colleague," "partner," "friend"
- Zoomorphic robots → "pet," "creature," "tool"
- Distributed systems → "system," "network," "swarm"
