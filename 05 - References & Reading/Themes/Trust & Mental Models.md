# Trust & Mental Models

How users build, calibrate, and repair trust in AI systems, and the cognitive models they construct about AI agency. Central to all three RQs.

---

## Core Papers

- [[Machines and Mindlessness — Nass 2000]] — humans apply social rules to computers automatically; "mindless" social responses
- [[Computers as Social Actors — Nass 1994]] — foundational CASA theory; computers trigger social cognition
- [[To Trust or to Think — Buçinca 2021]] — cognitive forcing functions reduce AI overreliance; trust calibration
- [[Trusting Virtual Agents — Zhou 2019]] — personality affects trust in virtual agents; empirical study
- [[Companion-Assistant Dynamics — Manoli 2025]] — "she's like a person but better"; mental models of AI companions
- [[Sycophantic AI — Cheng 2026]] — sycophancy decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence
- [[Personalising AI Assistance — Swaroop 2025]] — personalizing AI based on user's overreliance rate

## Key Concepts
- **CASA** (Computers As Social Actors) — automatic social responses to computers
- **Mental model** — user's internal representation of AI capability and intent
- **Overreliance** — trusting AI beyond its actual reliability
- **Trust calibration** — aligning trust to actual AI performance
- **Trust repair** — how trust is restored after AI failure (links to RQ3)

## Trust Repair (RQ3 specific)
These papers are most relevant to understanding what happens after an error:
- [[Machines and Mindlessness — Nass 2000]] — social attribution patterns
- [[To Trust or to Think — Buçinca 2021]] — failure modes of reliance
- [[Sycophantic AI — Cheng 2026]] — erosion of trust over time
