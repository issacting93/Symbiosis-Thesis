# AI Agency & Roles

Frameworks for understanding what AI does, how much autonomy it should have, and what "role" it plays in relation to the human. Relevant to **RQ1** (how users conceptualize AI agency) and **RQ2** (compliance and rapport).

---

## Core Papers

- [[Human-Automation Interaction Model — Parasuraman 2000]] — seminal taxonomy of automation levels; how much control humans delegate
- [[Human-AI Interaction Modes — Van Berkel 2021]] — intermittent, continuous, proactive AI modes
- [[Guidelines for HAI — Amershi 2019]] — Microsoft's 18 guidelines for human-AI interaction design
- [[Human-Centered AI — Shneiderman 2020]] — reliable, safe, trustworthy HCAI framework
- [[Emerging Roles Human-AI — Lindgren 2025]] — evolving role configurations between humans and AI
- [[Levels of Autonomy — Feng 2025]] — spectrum of AI agent autonomy
- [[Task-Driven Human-AI Collaboration — Afroogh 2026]] — when to automate, collaborate, or challenge
- [[Passive Tool to Teammate — Yan 2025]] — AI as socio-cognitive collaborator in learning
- [[Companionship to Mediationship — Wang 2025]] — AI role shift in caregiving contexts

## Autonomy Spectrum (Parasuraman)
```
1. Human does all → 2. Computer suggests → 3. Human selects → 4. Computer selects, human approves → ... → 10. Computer does all
```

## Implications for Embodiment Research
Embodiment modality changes *perceived* agency level:
- Humanoid robot → tends to be perceived as higher agency
- Distributed/swarm → lower perceived individual agency, higher perceived collective agency
- Virtual → variable, depends on interface design
