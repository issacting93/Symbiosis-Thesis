# Structural Holes — Burt 1992

**Authors:** Ronald S. Burt
**Year:** 1992
**Source:** Harvard University Press

---

## Summary
Argues that individuals who bridge otherwise disconnected social clusters — occupying the structural holes between groups — have informational and strategic advantages. Brokers access diverse information flows, can synthesise ideas across domains, and are well-positioned to recognise and act on novel opportunities. The value of a structural position is determined not by how many connections you have but by how many disconnected clusters you bridge.

## Relevance to Symbiosis
Theoretical grounding for the betweenness centrality argument. If AI accumulates betweenness centrality — sitting between human nodes, routing interactions that previously connected people directly — it appropriates the broker advantages that previously belonged to well-connected humans. The AI becomes the structural hole occupant; human brokers are displaced.
