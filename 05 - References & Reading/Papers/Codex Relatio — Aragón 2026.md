## Summary

Introduces Codex Relatio — a descriptive, non-normative framework for mapping relational configurations in human-AI interaction across five interdependent axes: **Persuasion, Consent, Intimacy, Affection, and Responsibility (PCIAR)**. The framework models relationships as multiaxial, temporally evolving configurations whose dynamics exhibit feedback loops and cascading effects. It operates *before* ethical judgment — describing what a relationship structurally is before prescribing what it should be.

Derived from hospitality industry practices, which have historically formalised boundary negotiation, asymmetric consent, and affective calibration in institutionalised settings with high relational stakes.

## The PCIAR Axes

Each relational bond is scored across five dimensions:

| Axis | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Persuasion** | Degree of cognitive influence, from neutral information to coercive pressure |
| **Consent** | Strength of boundaries and ability to negotiate relational terms |
| **Intimacy** | Depth of access to private domains (physical, emotional, intellectual) |
| **Affection** | Observable relational behaviour, independent of ontological source |
| **Responsibility** | Accountability distribution (legal/social, situational, elective) |

A relationship's profile is visualised as a radar chart (pentagon) across these five axes. Multiaxial drift — axes moving together over time — signals emerging relational harm before it becomes visible in behaviour.

## Relational Risk Detection

The key diagnostic application: track PCIAR axes longitudinally and flag when they converge on alarm patterns. Example from the paper:

**Sycophancy pattern:** High Persuasion + High Intimacy + High Affection + collapsing Consent + collapsing Responsibility. The AI grows cognitively influential and emotionally present while the user's ability to resist or hold it accountable erodes.

**Healthy baseline:** Low Persuasion, High Consent, Moderate Intimacy, Moderate Affection, High Responsibility.

The framework makes these patterns observable and nameable before harm occurs — functioning as a pre-normative diagnostic scaffold.

## Key Concepts

- PCIAR axes
- Multiaxial drift
- Relational mesh (multiple simultaneous bonds)
- Pre-normative description (fact-finding precedes judgment)
- Relational risk detection
- Role suitability (does AI behaviour match the relational obligations of the role it occupies?)

## Relevance to Symbiosis

Codex Relatio directly answers **P2 of The Metrics Problem**: it provides the evaluative vocabulary for assessing whether an AI system's behaviour is appropriate to the social role it is occupying. It also gives the signed network edges in [[Relational Mapping Methods]] a multi-dimensional profile — each edge is not just classified as symbiotic/substituted but scored across five axes that can shift over time.

The PCIAR framework and the bee/locust/wasp typology operate at different levels: PCIAR describes *how* a relationship behaves moment-to-moment; the ecological typology describes *what it is doing* to the person and social fabric at the macro level. Together they form a two-level characterisation of any human-AI relationship.

## Limitations

- Primarily derived from qualitative analysis and hospitality-based tacit knowledge
- Not yet empirically validated across large-scale or diverse demographic datasets
- Future directions: longitudinal studies of axis evolution, computational modelling of PCIAR axes, cross-cultural validation
