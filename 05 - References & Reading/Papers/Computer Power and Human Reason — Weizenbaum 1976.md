# Computer Power and Human Reason — Weizenbaum 1976

**Authors:** Joseph Weizenbaum  
**Year:** 1976  
**Venue:** W.H. Freeman (book)

---

## Summary
Weizenbaum reflects on ELIZA — a rule-based chatbot he built to simulate a Rogerian therapist — and his alarm at users forming genuine emotional attachments to it. Argues that computers should not be entrusted with decisions requiring genuine human understanding, empathy, or wisdom, even when they can convincingly simulate those qualities.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
The ELIZA effect is the earliest documented case of humans forming emotional bonds with rule-based systems. Establishes that anthropomorphic response does not require sophisticated AI — simple pattern-matching is sufficient to trigger social scripts. Directly relevant to understanding how minimal design cues produce outsized emotional responses.

## Key Concepts
- ELIZA effect
- Emotional attachment to machines
- Limits of simulation
- Human judgment vs. computation
