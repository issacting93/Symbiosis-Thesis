# Social Ecological Model — Bronfenbrenner 1979

**Authors:** Urie Bronfenbrenner
**Year:** 1979 (formalized); revised as PPCT model 1990s
**Source:** *The Ecology of Human Development*. Harvard University Press. / Wikipedia overview: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_ecological_model

---

## Summary
Framework for understanding human development as embedded in a set of nested environmental systems rather than as an individual process. Originally introduced by Chicago School sociologists after WWI, formalized by Bronfenbrenner in the 1970s–80s. The central claim: you cannot understand development by studying the individual in isolation — you must study the entire ecological system in which development occurs.

Later revised into the **Process-Person-Context-Time (PPCT)** model, which emphasizes "proximal processes" — regular, complex reciprocal interactions between a person and their environment — as the primary engines of development.

## Five Nested Systems

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **Microsystem** | Innermost layer — direct interactions (family, school, peers). Influences are strongest and bi-directional here |
| **Mesosystem** | Connections *between* microsystems — e.g. the relationship between a parent's workplace and a child's school |
| **Exosystem** | Larger social systems that affect development indirectly — settings the person is not directly part of but that shape their immediate environment |
| **Macrosystem** | The outermost cultural context — attitudes, norms, social conditions, institutional structures that cascade inward |
| **Chronosystem** | The temporal dimension — both external events and internal changes over time; frames all other systems historically |

## PPCT Model
- **Process** — proximal processes; regular, reciprocal interactions between person and environment
- **Person** — characteristics the person brings (demand, resource, force)
- **Context** — the four interconnected systems above
- **Time** — micro-, meso-, and macro-temporal dimensions

## Relevance to Symbiosis
The model directly supports the position paper's core methodological argument: that studying the individual in isolation misses where the most consequential effects occur. Bronfenbrenner's insistence on studying the "entire ecological system" is the theoretical foundation for why ego network methods are necessary — the individual session tells you about the microsystem; it says nothing about the mesosystem (the network of relationships around the person) or macrosystem (the cultural normalisation of AI substitution).

The nested systems map onto the two-track problem: AI may improve the microsystem interaction (task-level augmentation) while simultaneously degrading the mesosystem connections (social-level displacement). Standard metrics study only the microsystem.

The Chronosystem supports the argument for longitudinal study — the effects of AI on social networks are temporal patterns, not single-session events. Parasitism is a Chronosystem-level phenomenon.

The "proximal processes" concept parallels the scaffolding/substitution distinction: both frameworks ask whether regular, reciprocal interactions build capacity over time or replace it.

## Key Concepts
- Nested ecological systems
- Proximal processes
- Bi-directional influence
- Development as context-dependent
- Chronosystem / temporal dimension
- Microsystem vs. macrosystem
