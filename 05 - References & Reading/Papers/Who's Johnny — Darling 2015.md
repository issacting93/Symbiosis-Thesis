# Who's Johnny? — Darling 2015

**Author:** Kate Darling  
**Year:** 2015  
**Venue:** SSRN Electronic Journal  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2588669

---

## Summary
Examines anthropomorphic framing in human-robot interaction. Explores how naming, narrative backstory, and physical form lead people to treat robots as moral patients — even when they know the robot is a machine. Argues that anthropomorphic framing has real implications for HRI policy and design.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
One of the most cited papers bridging CASA theory to real-world HRI. Darling shows that anthropomorphism is not a bug to design away but a feature with design implications — particularly relevant for the zoomorphic and humanoid robot modalities in this research.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ1** — naming a robot changes the *linguistic metaphors* users employ ("my robot" vs "the device")
- **RQ2** — anthropomorphic framing increases trust and rapport with zoomorphic robots
- **RQ3** — users who anthropomorphize may show stronger blame attribution and emotional response to robot failure

## Key Concepts
