# System of Professions — Abbott 1988

**Authors:** Andrew Abbott  
**Year:** 1988  
**Venue:** University of Chicago Press (book)

---

## Summary
Abbott argues that professions are best understood not as fixed occupational groups but as competitors in an interdependent system, where jurisdiction — the legitimate claim to define, diagnose, and treat a class of problems — is always contestable and never permanently settled. New technologies don't simply eliminate professions; they redraw jurisdictional boundaries, creating vacancies that adjacent groups rush to occupy.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
Provides the sociological frame for understanding differential AI adoption across design, engineering, and product roles. When AI absorbs certain diagnostic or generative tasks, the question is not whether designers are displaced but which aspects of the work remain theirs and who else is now making credible jurisdictional claims on the rest.

## Key Concepts
- Jurisdictional claims
- Professional competition
- Division of expert labor
- Technology and professional boundary redrawing
- Vacancy chains
