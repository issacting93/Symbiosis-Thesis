# Bowling Alone — Putnam 2000

**Authors:** Robert D. Putnam
**Year:** 2000
**Source:** Simon & Schuster

---

## Summary
Documents the dramatic decline in social capital across American society since the 1960s — civic participation, informal socialising, trust in institutions, and community engagement all declining across decades. Identifies television, commuting, and generational change as partial causes. Argues that social capital — the networks, norms, and trust embedded in relationships — enables cooperation and produces collective value that cannot be replaced by individual resources.

## Relevance to Symbiosis
Provides the macro social context for the research. If AI displaces human-human relationships at scale, it contributes to a long-running decline in social capital that Putnam documents. The framework proposed in the Social Dynamics study provides a mechanism-level account of how this decline occurs through individual adoption patterns aggregating into structural change.
