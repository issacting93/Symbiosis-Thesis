# Computers as Social Actors — Nass 1994

**Authors:** Clifford Nass, Jonathan Steuer, Ellen R. Tauber  
**Year:** 1994  
**Venue:** CHI  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/191666.191703

---

## Summary
Establishes the **CASA** (Computers As Social Actors) framework. Demonstrates that users apply social rules — politeness, attribution of personality, gender stereotypes — to computers, even when they know computers are not human. This is not "foolishness" but a fundamental feature of human social cognition.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
The foundational paper explaining *why* embodiment form matters. If people automatically anthropomorphize, then the *type* of form (virtual, humanoid, zoomorphic) will trigger different social scripts and mental models. CASA explains the mechanism behind RQ1 and RQ2.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ1** — CASA explains why mental models of AI will vary with embodiment: different forms trigger different social schemas
- **RQ2** — social rules (trust, rapport, compliance) apply automatically to AI; embodiment amplifies or suppresses this
- **RQ3** — blame attribution toward AI follows social attribution patterns, not technical ones

## Key Concepts
