# Human-Automation Interaction Model — Parasuraman 2000

**Authors:** R. Parasuraman, T.B. Sheridan, C.D. Wickens  
**Year:** 2000  
**Venue:** IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/3468.844354

---

## Summary
Proposes a comprehensive model of automation with two dimensions: (1) **types** of functions that can be automated (information acquisition, analysis, decision selection, action implementation) and (2) **levels** of automation from fully manual to fully autonomous. Creates a 10-level scale of human delegation to machines.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
This is the foundational framework for understanding what "automation level" means. The four embodiment types in Symbiosis research map onto different points on this scale — distributed/swarm AI tends toward higher levels of automation; virtual AI tends toward decision-support (middle levels); humanoid robots often operate at mixed levels.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ1** — automation level correlates with perceived agency; higher automation = AI perceived as more agent-like
- **RQ2** — willingness to comply is related to where AI sits on the automation scale
- **RQ3** — blame attribution depends on who "made the decision" — automation level affects this

## Key Concepts
- Levels of automation (1–10 scale)
- Types of automation (acquisition, analysis, selection, implementation)
- Human supervisory control
- Complacency and skill degradation
- Situation awareness in automation

## Automation Level Scale
```
1  Human does everything
2  Computer offers alternatives
3  Computer suggests one alternative
4  Computer executes suggestion if human approves
5  Computer executes, human has brief window to veto
6  Computer executes, then notifies human
7  Computer executes, notifies human only if asked
8  Computer executes and ignores human
9  Computer executes and tells human only when it decides
10 Computer decides everything
```
