# To Trust or to Think — Buçinca 2021

**Authors:** Zana Buçinca, Maja Barbara Malaya, Krzysztof Z. Gajos  
**Year:** 2021  
**Venue:** CSCW (ACM PACMHCI)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3449287

---

## Summary
Examines overreliance on AI in decision-making and tests "cognitive forcing functions" — design interventions that interrupt automatic trust and prompt deliberate evaluation. Finds that design can reduce overreliance without sacrificing efficiency.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
The symbiosis model requires a *calibrated* relationship — not parasitic over-dependence, not fearful rejection. Buçinca et al. show that design can actively cultivate appropriate trust levels. This is the empirical backbone for the "non-parasitic" design goal.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ2** — shows that compliance (willingness to act on AI recommendation) is designable, not fixed
- **RQ3** — after errors, users may over-correct (no trust) or under-correct (continued over-trust); forcing functions help
- **RQ1** — implicit: mental models of AI reliability shape when people trust

## Key Concepts
- Overreliance on AI
- Cognitive forcing functions
- AI-assisted decision-making
- Trust calibration
- System 1 vs. System 2 thinking in human-AI interaction
