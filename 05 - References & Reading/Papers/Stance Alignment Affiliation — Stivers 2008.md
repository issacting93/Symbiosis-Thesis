# Stance, Alignment, and Affiliation — Stivers 2008

**Author:** Tanya Stivers  
**Year:** 2008  
**Venue:** Research on Language and Social Interaction  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/08351810701691123

---

## Summary
Examines how nodding functions as a nonverbal token of affiliation during storytelling. Distinguishes between *alignment* (sequential participation) and *affiliation* (displaying shared stance). Shows that even minimal nonverbal behavior carries substantial social meaning.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
Central to the nonverbal interaction dimension of the project. Stivers demonstrates that a simple nod is not just acknowledgment — it's a stance-taking act. This is exactly the kind of nonverbal behavior that current AI cannot interpret, and that the research group is exploring.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ1** — nonverbal signals like nodding, gesture, and expression constitute a separate channel of agency-signaling and mental model construction
- **RQ2** — affiliation signals (or their absence) in embodied AI may affect rapport

## Key Concepts
- Stance / stance-taking
- Alignment vs. affiliation (distinct concepts)
- Nodding as social action
- Nonverbal communication in conversation analysis
- Minimal responses

## The Research Gap This Points To
If AI could *receive* nodding/gesture/affiliation signals, it could respond to stance as well as content. This opens the door to embodiment-specific interaction (humanoid robots can nod back; distributed systems cannot).
