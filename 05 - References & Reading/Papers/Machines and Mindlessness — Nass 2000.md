# Machines and Mindlessness — Nass 2000

**Authors:** Clifford Nass, Youngme Moon  
**Year:** 2000  
**Venue:** Journal of Social Issues  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-4537.00153

---

## Summary
Follow-up to the 1994 CASA paper. Shows that social responses to computers are "mindless" — people apply social rules even when explicitly told the computer is "just a machine." Demonstrates politeness effects, gender stereotyping of voice interfaces, and team identity effects.

## Why It Matters for Symbiosis
Extends CASA: social responses are *automatic and robust*, not based on confusion. This means that embodiment design choices (form, voice, gender) reliably trigger specific social schemas regardless of user sophistication — critical for predicting how different embodiment types will affect mental models.

## Relevance to RQs
- **RQ1** — "mindless" social schemas provide predictable templates for how language and metaphor will differ by embodiment type
- **RQ2** — trust and rapport emerge from automatic, not deliberate, social responses
- **RQ3** — blame attribution will also be mindless — people attribute blame using human moral schemas

## Key Concepts
- Mindlessness in social interaction
- Automatic social responses
- Politeness toward computers
- Gender stereotyping of voice
- Team identity with technology
