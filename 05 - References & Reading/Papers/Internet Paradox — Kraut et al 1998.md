# Internet Paradox — Kraut et al 1998

**Authors:** Robert Kraut, Michael Patterson, Vicki Lundmark, Sara Kiesler, Tridas Mukopadhyay, William Scherlis
**Year:** 1998
**Source:** American Psychologist, 53(9), 1017–1031

---

## Summary
Longitudinal study following new internet users over one to two years. Found that greater internet use was associated with decreased communication with family members in the household, smaller social networks, and increased loneliness and depression — despite the internet being explicitly designed for social connection. The finding was counterintuitive and contested; subsequent follow-up studies found effects diminished as users developed social uses of the technology over time.

## Relevance to Symbiosis
The direct methodological and empirical precedent for this study. Establishes the approach — longitudinal tracking of technology use alongside social network quality — and the core paradox this research updates for AI: social technology that reduces social connection. The AI-era version of this question involves tools that are more capable, more personalised, and more relationally engaging than early internet use.
