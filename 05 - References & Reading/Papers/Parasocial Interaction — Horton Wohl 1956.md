# Parasocial Interaction — Horton Wohl 1956

**Authors:** Donald Horton, Richard Wohl
**Year:** 1956
**Source:** Psychiatry, 19(3), 215–229

---

## Summary
Introduces the concept of parasocial interaction — the one-sided sense of intimacy that audiences develop with media figures (talk show hosts, actors, news presenters) who do not know them. Viewers apply social scripts: they feel directly addressed, respond emotionally to the figure's apparent mood, and may feel grief at the relationship's end. The relationship is experienced as real despite being structurally one-sided.

## Relevance to Symbiosis
Foundational theory for AI companion and assistant relationships. AI companion relationships are structurally parasocial — the AI does not have the relationship the user believes it has. The parasocial escalation literature maps directly onto the temporal displacement dimension: as parasocial intensity increases, it competes with and may displace real social relationships. CASA and Media Equation research builds on this foundation.
