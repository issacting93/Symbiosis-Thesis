# Strength of Weak Ties — Granovetter 1973

**Authors:** Mark Granovetter
**Year:** 1973
**Source:** American Journal of Sociology, 78(6), 1360–1380

---

## Summary
Counterintuitive finding that weak ties — relationships with distant acquaintances and peripheral connections — are more valuable than strong ties for information access, job opportunities, and social integration. Strong ties (close friends, family) reinforce existing knowledge and social circles; weak ties bridge otherwise disconnected clusters and provide access to novel information. Weak tie networks are structurally essential for social mobility and information diversity.

## Relevance to Symbiosis
Identifies which social connections AI adoption is most likely to displace first. Weak ties are easier to substitute — a quick question to a peripheral contact can be replaced by a search or a chatbot more readily than a close mentoring relationship. If AI primarily displaces weak ties, it may quietly collapse the bridges between social clusters that provide access to diverse ideas and opportunities.
