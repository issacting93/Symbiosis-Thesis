#### **Technology, Mediation, and Behavior Change**

* Core framework: “the things we design, design us” (Tony Fry, Marty Willis, drawing on Heidegger)  
  * Technologies embed ontological and normative claims: how the world is, and how it should be  
  * These claims carry political and ethical stakes, benefiting some populations over others  
* AI changes practices, establishes new norms, and habituates users silently and faster than noticed  
  * Deeper effects: cognition, memory, empathy, theory of mind  
  * Emerging area of interest: AI psychosis, theorizing psychotic structures in relation to AI

#### **Nonverbal AI Interaction: Opportunities and Concerns**

* Affective computing and facial recognition raise significant bias issues  
  * Accuracy drops for darker skin tones and female-presenting faces  
  * Misidentification used by police and government agencies, disproportionately harms people of color and women  
  * Improving accuracy requires more surveillance data, which creates its own ethical problem  
* Sketching and drawing as nonverbal creative process: literature from Malcolm McCullough (*Abstracting Craft*) and Gabriella Goldschmidt on move-making and reflective practice  
  * CAD tools changed but did not eliminate craft; AI poses a similar but faster disruption  
  * Concern: students sketch less; moving to tools too early undermines design thinking development  
* What excites about nonverbal AI: using it as a creative tool to prototype and materialize ideas faster, especially in areas where skills are limited (e.g., development)  
* What terrifies: AI identifying, recognizing, or surveilling individuals

#### **UX Design Education and Professional Standards**

* UX as a profession is only \~20 years old, with no established benchmarks or standards  
  * Graduate designers sit alongside bootcamp graduates (e.g., Google/General Assembly, 4-6 months) doing the same jobs  
  * “Design thinking” diluted the field further: if anyone can do it, specialist value erodes  
* AI is not the root cause of UX’s identity crisis; it predates AI and has been ignored for 25 years  
  * AI has accelerated role collapse: designers now expected to do research, development, strategy, and management  
  * Contrast with engineering: AI is an existential threat to engineers; for UX, the field lacked definition to begin with  
* Current state: “the field is an absolute disaster and we’re in a world of trouble”
