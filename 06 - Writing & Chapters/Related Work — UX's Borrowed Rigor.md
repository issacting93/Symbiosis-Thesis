
**Past HCI methods**

I think one big issue UIUX has today is related to how it came to be.
UIUX design borrowed a lot of knowledge from other schools, social sciences, psychology, architecture, engineering, etc, but did not inherit the discipline required to create new knowledge. So as times change, a lot of the tools and methods did not have sufficient depth to develop into new skills. 

While at the same time, UX inherited a lot of the values that came from these schools, ( ethics, human centric, accessibility) without necessarily the rigor or understanding of where these ideas came from, and more importantly how to engage with them in a new context.

What helped build UX as its own field was people like Don Norman translating ideas from cog science into a language that is understandable and applicable to day to day. Those ideas, while still largely relevant, needs to be updated to help us engage with a world even more entangled with technology and its impact on our relationships. The way to do it is not do drive designers into engineering but rather science. Scieñe, which teaches us how to observe the world, to describe it in a shared language, to understand that observation and to share it with our community.
To understand the world , we need to engage with sciences. To understand ourselves and each other, we need to engage with the humanities, our humanity and all the things that make life worth living.

My advisor always said, science needs to be built on science.

Now more than anything we need designers to engage with science and scientists to learn how our work impacts this complex world.

Technology has changed at such a rapid, unsustainable rate that there are definitely consequences to come. We are gonna need deeper, richer data, that can only be collected through an ethnographic method , studied in a qualitative manner to translate the lived human experiences into a few small but important insights that can help us understand better what we should build, or not build.
Big data is very good at measuring things, but it will never be able to tell us what it means.

Meaning can only be derived from those who have lived. A machine doesn't, and probably will never understand meaning.

Perhaps the corporations dont understand the value of crafting. We need it anyway.
It is only through a process of creative exploration that we can distill our instincts, insights and ideas into experiences that someone else would enjoy.

They don't have to understand why we do it.

I have learned that you cannot explain why craft is important, or the process of taking time crafting, to people who don't already see the value to it. Many of them have already convinced themselves that disengaging with their own humanity is a good way to live life.
