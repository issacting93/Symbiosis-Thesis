## Page 2 — Instrumental, Relational, Prescribed, Emergent

### Two axes

```
Instrumental ←————————————→ Relational
                    ↕
         Prescribed behavior
                    ↕
         Emergent behavior
```

- **Instrumental** — using AI as a tool to get something done
- **Relational** — forming a relationship with AI as an entity
- **Prescribed behavior** — the system determines how you behave
- **Emergent behavior** — behavior arises from the interaction, unpredictable

### Three questions about Spike Jonze / *Her*

1. Did he see the technology and imagine a future?
2. Did big tech watch his film — and did someone decide to build the tech?
3. Did he examine his life and the human condition?

→ The third is the real answer. *Her* works because it examined something true about people, not because it predicted technology.

### The research/design loop

```
Sense → Observe → Insight → Inspire → Visualise → Build → Implement → Observe...
                                                  ↓
                                            Research → Design → Engineer
```

### The automating question

**What exactly are we automating when we bring AI into the picture?**

This is the question that sits underneath the whole project. When a tool automates a task, it changes what the human does. When AI automates a *relationship function* (conversation, empathy, companionship), what does the human stop doing?

## Page 3 — Ethnography, the Matrix, and Prescribed vs. Emergent

### Opening move

**How do we better understand our world?**
→ Ethnography
→ To understand the relationships and how they affect us
→ How do we know?

### The matrix

A first attempt at mapping who uses AI, why, and what emerges:

| Emotional state | Identity | Tool | Relationship that emerges |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lonely | — | Dating apps, Replika, ChatGPT | Romance / companionship |
| Anxious | Designer | Figma, AI tools | Coworker / collaborator |

*The left column is the emotional driver. The tool is selected to address it. The relationship is what forms — often unintended.*

**The observation:** People do not pick tools neutrally. They pick them from a felt need, and the relationship that forms with the tool is shaped by that need. A lonely person and a stressed designer both use ChatGPT — but they are in fundamentally different relationships with it.

### Prescribed vs. Emergent — the key distinction

```
Tools → prescribed behavior
Relationships → emergent behavior
```

- A **tool** tells you what to do — its interface prescribes the interaction
- A **relationship** produces emergent behavior — neither party fully controls it

**The problem with AI:** it looks like a tool (chat interface, prescribed inputs) but produces emergent, relational dynamics. The interface is instrumental. The effect is relational. That gap is where parasitism hides.

→ This is the mechanism: AI is designed as a tool (prescribed) but activates the relational cognitive system (emergent). The user's brain responds as if to a relationship. The product is optimised as if it is a tool.

## Connections to the Symbiosis Framework

- The **prescribed/emergent** distinction maps directly onto the symbiosis argument: parasitic AI exploits emergent relational dynamics while maintaining the plausible deniability of being "just a tool"
- The **matrix** is a prototype for the field research method — mapping emotional state → tool choice → emergent relationship type is exactly what ethnographic fieldwork could surface
- **"What are we automating?"** is the central ethical question for the framework: automating a task vs. automating a relational function are different in kind
- **Not all users adapt at the same level** — this is the inequality argument: the risks of parasitic AI are not evenly distributed. Those with fewer social resources are more vulnerable
